Problem Space 3: Transnational Households

The problem

Money sent home often has to become cash at a pickup counter before it can buy groceries or medicine. Many recipients have a phone but unreliable or costly mobile data. Senders cannot easily earmark support for essentials, and neighbourhood shops carry informal credit risk.

Our solution

A remittance becomes a shared family spending balance held by a Soroban smart contract. The family member pays at participating local shops by approving each purchase on their phone, without internet. The online shop submits the approval; the contract pays the shop in one atomic transaction.

How a purchase works

Sender · onlineFundsthe family balance
Shop · onlineInvoice QRlocal price + exact charge
Family phone · offlineSignsthat exact purchase
Shop · onlineSubmitsthe signed approval
StellarVerifies + paysonce; replays refused

Offline authorization

The phone holds an Ed25519 key registered on chain by the sender. Approving signs a fixed 340-byte message binding the Testnet network, the contract, the token, family, shop, terminal, a random invoice ID, the exact amount, the quoted local price and an expiry. The contract rebuilds those bytes and verifies the signature: change any field and it fails.

Why Stellar

  • Soroban enforces the rules on chain: one-time invoices, per-family balances, key revocation, merchant binding.
  • Stellar Asset Contracts let the contract hold and pay a dollar-denominated token.
  • Low fees and ~5 s finality make per-purchase settlement practical.
  • Native Ed25519 verification: the family member needs no Stellar account and no XLM.

What makes it different

  • vs. remittance apps: spendable at the shop with no cash pickup.
  • vs. gift cards: one balance across independent shops; rules enforced by a public contract.
  • vs. crypto wallets: the shopper needs no data, no XLM, no crypto knowledge.
